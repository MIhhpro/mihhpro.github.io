## 1. When something goes wrong

Use this procedure for a lost phone/computer, suspicious account access, a wrongly shared document or message, or lost/altered client records. It covers Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Calendly, Meet, Messenger, WhatsApp, Telegram, Viber, Signal and relevant billing records. Add the planned iPad when it starts handling client data.

1. **Write down what happened and when you discovered it.** Start the blank log below. Distinguish suspicion from the point when there is reasonable certainty that personal data was compromised. Investigate promptly; do not wait for every detail before recognising a breach.
2. **Stop further exposure using a trusted device.** Follow the relevant scenario below. Pause affected sharing or client communications if continuing would expose more information.
3. **Preserve minimal evidence.** Record alerts, times, permissions and relevant access history if available. Keep only what is necessary to understand the incident; avoid copying whole chats or medical papers. Do not delay containment to collect evidence or wipe all evidence reflexively.
4. **Assess the effect on people and make the notification decision immediately.** Reassess as facts emerge. A provider investigation or a lawyer's availability does not suspend the reporting clock.
5. **Restore safe access, verify the fix and document the outcome.** Do not resume use of a compromised device until it is safe.

Awareness and notification rules: [EDPB Guidelines 9/2022, sections II.A and V](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-04/edpb_guidelines_202209_personal_data_breach_notification_v2.0_en.pdf).

## 2. Actions for common situations

| Situation | Immediate actions | Checks before closing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lost or stolen device | From a trusted device, use available remote locking and account session-revocation controls. Secure accounts reachable from the device, including email recovery routes. If appropriate, arrange SIM blocking with the mobile provider. Consider remote erasure if necessary to protect the data; document the decision and evidence limitations. | Establish whether storage was encrypted, the device was locked, sessions remained open and data could be recovered safely. A screen lock alone does not prove there was no exposure. Also assess loss of access to the only copy. |
| Compromised email or other account | Use the provider's genuine account-recovery/security process. Change compromised or reused passwords, end unrecognised sessions, review recovery methods and two-step verification. Check unauthorised app access, email forwarding/filter changes and sharing changes. | Check the affected period and connected services: a compromised Google account could expose Drive, Calendar and booking messages. Verify recovery details and permissions. Do not communicate sensitive recovery information through an account still under an attacker's control. |
| Wrong Drive recipient or public link | Remove the incorrect permission or link access immediately. Check both file and parent-folder access. Record what was available and for how long. Contact the unintended recipient privately to request deletion and no further sharing, where appropriate. | Revoking access cannot retrieve an already downloaded copy or screenshot. Record any evidence of access and any deletion confirmation; absence of an access log is not proof that nobody viewed it. |
| Email/chat attachment sent to the wrong person | Use available recall/deletion controls promptly, without assuming they work for every recipient or copy. Request deletion and no forwarding through an appropriate channel. Do not resend the attachment as evidence. | Identify what was actually sent, who received it and any indication of viewing or forwarding. A recipient's deletion assurance informs the assessment but does not erase the incident. |
| Missing or altered client records | Stop relying on affected training or health notes until accuracy is established. Check available version/recovery history without creating a new uncontrolled archive. Reconfirm essential information with the client through a safe channel if needed. | Check both the confidentiality and the practical effects of loss or alteration. Do not assume a provider has a recoverable backup. |
| Provider reports an incident | Preserve the provider's notice, identify which of your clients/data may be affected and request necessary information through official support. Assess your own obligations rather than assuming the provider has handled them. | Track updates, affected dates and remedial actions. Involve the accountant or billing provider only where the incident concerns their access or records. |

For medical links or Meet messages, assess the actual document permissions and exposure. A meeting ending or a chat disappearing does not establish that linked files or all copies were deleted.

## 3. Decide who needs to be informed

Consider the data involved, who could access it, whether people can be identified, exposure duration, recovery and possible harm. Health information, medication, client photos, home addresses and information about minors deserve particular attention. Consider distress, identity misuse, discrimination and unsafe coaching caused by altered notes. Even a single affected client can face serious harm. Document the facts supporting the decision, including uncertainties.

| Finding | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| Investigation finds no personal-data breach | Record the facts and why, complete any security fix and reopen if new evidence emerges. |
| A breach is unlikely to create a risk to people's rights and freedoms | Document the breach and the reasons for not notifying the authority. |
| A breach does not meet that unlikely-risk exception | Notify the competent supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours after becoming aware**. |
| A breach is likely to create a **high risk** | Also notify affected people **without undue delay**, clearly explaining what happened, likely consequences, measures taken and a contact point. |

Record breaches even when no external notification is needed. Exceptions to informing individuals, such as effective encryption that kept exposed data unintelligible, require evidence and an individual assessment; do not assume a password or later link removal is sufficient. [EDPB small-business breach guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/assess-the-risks/data-breaches_en).

For this Hungarian business, start with the [NAIH incident-reporting page](https://www.naih.hu/adatvedelmi-incidensbejelento-rendszer) and its current electronic reporting routes. This is a controller's breach notification, not a general complaint. Count elapsed hours, including weekends, rather than business days. Do not treat 72 hours as a waiting period. If reporting is late, explain the delay. If details are incomplete, provide what is known and follow up without undue further delay; keep the submission receipt and reference. Seek prompt specialist help for an uncertain high-risk case or additional obligations involving overseas clients, without postponing necessary action.

Prepare the report from the log: nature of the breach, categories and approximate numbers of people and records, contact point, likely consequences, and measures taken or planned. Distinguish estimates from confirmed facts. Do not attach an entire client medical history merely to demonstrate that health data was involved. [NAIH reporting instructions and forms](https://www.naih.hu/adatvedelmi-incidensbejelento-rendszer).

Send client notices individually through a verified safe channel. If business email is compromised, first secure it or use an existing verified alternative; never expose other clients through a group recipient list. For minors, assess the appropriate child/guardian communication and authority to receive information. A general website announcement is not the default substitute for direct notice.

## 6. Handling a client's data request

The owner confirms he can locate a client's information across the services he uses. Use the following procedure for access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection or consent withdrawal; practical account steps still need testing.

1. Log receipt, scope and the response deadline. Accept a clear request received through an existing contact channel; do not insist on a special form. Respond without undue delay, normally within one calendar month. An extension of up to two further months may be justified by complexity or number of requests; explain it within the first month.
2. Verify identity proportionately if there is reasonable doubt. For a representative, establish authority and scope before disclosure. Do not routinely collect identity documents or assume parenthood gives unrestricted access to every record.
3. Check the relevant email, Drive documents, Calendar/Calendly entries, chat apps, permitted device copies and billing/publication-permission records. Do not recreate already deleted data or collect new medical records to answer the request.
4. For access, provide the client's personal data and required explanatory information, protecting other people's rights. For correction/deletion/restriction, act across the records you control and notify recipients where required. Assess any statutory retention or specific claims exception individually; do not erase legally required billing records automatically.
5. Deliver the result securely to the verified person, without exposing other clients. Explain any limitation, legal reason, retention period or criterion, and complaint/judicial remedies. Keep a minimal completion record, not a duplicate medical archive.

Requests are normally free. These deadlines and safeguards follow the [EDPB guidance on individuals' rights](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en); they are separate from the coaching reply promise.

Withdrawal of publication permission needs prompt action: stop further use, remove the approved content from controlled website/social channels and record the affected items and completion. Do not wait a month simply because a general request deadline exists. Restrict any minimal evidence that must remain; explain provider or third-party copies outside your control accurately.

For erasure, check sharing, linked files, permitted downloads and available trash/deletion controls, while preserving only justified exceptions. Do not equate removing a link with deleting its target, or a sender deleting a chat message with guaranteed removal everywhere. Never promise erasure of independent provider records you cannot control.

Private request log fields: reference; receipt date/channel; identity/authority check outcome; scope; deadline; services checked; actions; exceptions and reasons; recipients contacted; response date/method; completion and record-retention review. Do not store identity-document copies by default.

## 7. Put the procedure into use

Before relying on it, rehearse a fictional wrong-recipient link incident and a fictional data request, without using real client records or sending notices. Confirm safe account-recovery access, the relevant sharing/deletion controls and a private log location. This rehearsal and account checks have not been performed by the assistant. Revisit the guide after an incident or a change of device, account plan or provider.
